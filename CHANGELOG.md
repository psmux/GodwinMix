# Changelog

## Unreleased

* Deinterlacing on an NVIDIA card stays on the card. GStreamer has no CUDA deinterlacer, so until now each interlaced picture went down to system memory for YADIF on the CPU and came back up. On a headend of fifty broadcast channels that was about nine cores of YADIF and five more of copies. A kernel of GodwinMix's own now does it in CUDA memory, loaded as PTX by the driver, so it needs no NVRTC and works on every card from Maxwell on. Measured on a 1080i channel it takes 0.08 of a core against 0.37, and scores within 0.15 VMAF of YADIF. `GMX_CUDA_DEINTERLACE=off` puts it back on the CPU.

## 0.3.20 (2026-10-29)

Sound filters, built in.

* Eight filters work on sound: gain, EQ (a low cut, bass, middle and treble, and up to ten bands of your own), noise gate, expander, compressor, limiter, delay and noise suppression (RNNoise, in pure Rust). Each runs in place on the thread that carries the samples, with no thread or copy of its own: under 0.1% of one core each on an Apple silicon MacBook, and about 1% for noise suppression. They go on a source or on the programme with `filter.add`, or in `[[filters]]`. See [Clean up a microphone](docs/how-to/clean-up-a-microphone.md) and [the reference](docs/reference/sound-filters.md).
* Each channel of the Audio panel has an FX button: add, set, switch off and remove filters live, or press Voice for a low cut, a gate, a compressor and a limiter in one go.
* An OBS import brings each source's gain, noise gate, noise suppression, compressor, limiter, expander and three band equaliser across with their settings. They used to arrive under GodwinMix names that nothing ran. They now stay on their source, as in OBS, and are added with it. OBS's video delay is no longer mistaken for a sound delay.
* A filter added from the page or the API is kept in the runtime store and comes back when the mixer restarts, and when a stalled source is rebuilt. Before, both lost it.
* `filter.set` changes only the settings it names, as its description says. It used to reset the others to their defaults.
* A second filter on the same source or the same point of the programme now runs after the first. It used to fail to link, and removing a filter from the middle of a chain relinked the wrong pair.
* A sound filter on a source's input side went nowhere: it was linked to the proxy below the audio tee, which the capsfilter above was never linked to. It now goes in above the tee.
* `filter.list` answers with each filter's settings.

Audio buses.

* The programme mix has a master fader and mute (`audio.master.set`, `[audio.master]`), kept across restarts in the runtime store like a source's fader. At unity it costs nothing.
* Buses: mixes of their own beside the programme, each with a master, a mute and a meter. An ordinary bus carries each source at the level of its send (`audio.send.set`, `sends` in `[[audio.bus]]`), after the source's fader and following its mute. Nothing is built for buses until the first one is added, and the last one to go leaves the programme pipeline as it was. A bus goes in and comes out live with no gap in the programme's sound.
* A mix minus is a bus with `minus = "guest-1"`: the programme as heard, following every take and crossfade, without that one source. It is a bus option so the guest invites can ask for one per guest.
* Cue and solo: a bus with `cue = true` carries the sources marked with `audio.cue.set`, muted ones too, and the programme while none is marked. `solo` clears every other mark. Nothing on air changes.
* A bus plays on a sound card on the mixer's machine (`device`, by name, id or number from `audio.device.list`, or `default`), in a pipeline of its own so a card that fails costs that bus only. It can be heard in a browser at `/pcm/bus:<id>` and `/opus/bus:<id>`.
* An output takes its sound from a bus with `audio = "<bus id>"`, through an encoder for that bus that runs while an output reads it. Left out, it is the programme's sound as before.
* The audio panel has a master strip, a bus picker with the picked bus's fader, mute, meter, sound card and a listen button, a form to add a bus, and Cue, Solo and a send fader on each source.
* `event/meters` carries each bus's peaks in `buses`. New methods `audio.master.set`, `audio.bus.list`, `audio.bus.add`, `audio.bus.set`, `audio.bus.remove`, `audio.send.set`, `audio.cue.set` and `audio.device.list`, with agent tools behind `search_tools`. See [Mix audio with buses](docs/how-to/mix-audio-with-buses.md) and [the reference](docs/reference/audio-buses.md).

A teleprompter, built in. vMix and Wirecast have none, and OBS needs a plugin.

* Scripts are kept on the station by slug id, each with a title and its words. A line that starts with `#` is a marker to jump to, and `*italic*` and `**bold**` are drawn. They live in `godwinmix.prompter.json` beside the config and come back after a restart, paused where the prompter last stopped.
* `/prompter/` is the screen: a phone, a tablet or the monitor under the glass, full screen, with white words on black and a cue line. Any number of screens follow one state. Each scrolls by itself from the position, the moment it was true on the mixer's clock and the speed, so the mixer sends nothing while the words move. Two screens of 390 and 1920 pixels read 0.004 rows apart at the same moment.
* The mixer breaks the text into rows at one width in characters for every screen, which is also the text size: every screen fits that many characters across, so a row on a phone is the same words as a row on the monitor.
* Keys on the screen: Space plays and pauses, the arrows change the speed, Page Down and Page Up go between markers, which is what most foot pedals and clickers send, M mirrors this screen and F is full screen. A tap brings up the same buttons. A screen opened with a read token follows and says it cannot drive.
* Mirror for beam splitter glass is set from the desk for every screen, and a screen can override it for itself.
* View > Prompter is a desk panel to write and pick scripts and to drive the prompter while live: play, speed, markers, a slider for the place, the text size, margins, the cue line and the mirror. **Open on a screen** shows a code for a phone, carrying a read token made for it.
* New methods: `prompter.get`, `prompter.rows`, `prompter.script.list`, `get`, `save` and `remove`, `prompter.load`, `play`, `pause`, `speed`, `seek` and `set`, and the events `prompter.changed` and `prompter.script.changed`, sent only to a client that subscribes to them. `prompter.speed` carries on from where the prompter is, so a plugin that follows the voice can send it as often as it likes. The agent tools are behind `search_tools`, and the Companion module has five prompter actions. See [Use the teleprompter](docs/how-to/use-the-teleprompter.md) and [the reference](docs/reference/prompter.md).

## 0.3.19 (2026-10-28)

* A converted channel whose encoder failed twice in a row, as one NVENC encoder did when fifty channels started at once, sent nothing until its show was stopped and started again. The second failure came inside the five seconds between two builds of a node, so the node was never built again; and the failure stopped the decode's stream, whose input then filled and held the stream's thread for good. A node that fails again too soon is now built again once the five seconds are over, and the thread never waits on a decoder: one that takes nothing more loses whole GOPs at its own input instead. A converted pair whose name was still held by the stream's last session is tried again a second later instead of never.
* A show whose output keeps trying to connect while its input is live is in alarm (`output-failed`, with how long and why) once a minute has passed, and the station's log says so once. Its health read `ok` the whole time before. An HLS output that loses its connection waits 1, 2, 4 up to 16 s between tries, and 1 s again once it has been live.

* A converted SD picture stored at 704x480 and shown at 16:9 came out squeezed, about 1056 wide between black bars, so people looked thin. The caps the MPEG-2 decoder was given said nothing of the picture's shape, so NVDEC called its pixels square and the scaler fitted the stored size. They now carry the pixel aspect from the sequence header, both scalers fit by the shape shown, a 16:9 source fills 1280x720 and a 4:3 one is laid in 960 wide, and an advert that switches between them is fitted again at the switch. `video.aspect` (`auto`, `16:9`, `4:3`) overrides the stream for a broadcaster that flags its picture wrong.
* Deinterlacing works on the GPU path. The picture goes down in I420 for the CPU's deinterlace and back up, and the scale and the encode stay on the card, since GStreamer has no GPU deinterlace and its NVDEC decoders always weave. `video.deinterlace_method` is `yadif` (the default), `greedyh` or `linear`. One deinterlace of a source serves every rendition of it that asks the same way. Measured, a 1080i channel costs about 9% of one core and a 480i one about 5%.
* A Flussonic `deinterlace=false` is read as `auto` unless `[headend] flussonic_deinterlace = "file"`, and `headend.import` takes the same choice. Flussonic's false scaled both fields as one frame and combed on every movement; `auto` passes a progressive picture untouched.
* A 1080i source converted at 1080 never ran: with no scale in front of it, the encoder was handed the decoder's `mixed` interlaced caps, which NVENC refuses. Every encoder now takes woven frames.
* `video.match_source` (`{max_height, bitrate_kbps}`) converts a source taller than the rendition at its own height up to `max_height`: one profile makes 1080p of an HD source and 720p of the rest. Two built in profiles use it, `h264-hd-1080p-else-720p` and `h264-hd-1080p-720p-ladder`.
* A direct show's HLS output carries a ladder: each rung a variant of the master playlist, decoded and deinterlaced once, the sound the first rung's, and its MPEG-TS beside the CMAF. A profile's `ladder` adds rungs under its picture, a Flussonic `transcoder` line with several `vb` and `size` imports as one, `headend.export` writes one, and the Flussonic URLs serve each rung as `tracks-v2a1` and on.
* A headend channel keeps its profile and can carry `video_overrides` of its own (shape, deinterlace and method, fit), set on the channel editor, in a bulk edit of the channel list and in `headend.channel.bulk`.

## 0.3.18 (2026-10-28)

A headend is set up in GodwinMix itself. The headend screen used to offer one way in, importing a Flussonic config; it now has three, and the import is one of them.

* With no channels, the headend screen offers Add a channel, Scan a multicast feed and Import a Flussonic config. The band keeps a button for each, and one for the channel list.
* Add, edit, duplicate and remove channels on the screen: a name (the public path), an input (UDP or RTP multicast or unicast MPEG-TS with its group, port, card and program number, SRT, RTMP, RTSP, an HLS pull or a file) with an optional backup, a conversion (pass through, a profile, or settings of its own), the HLS output and any UDP, SRT or RTMP outputs, and on or off. The channel list changes many at once: a profile, on or off, the network card, or removal.
* Scan a multicast feed: give the group, port and card, and the station lists every programme with its name, its picture size and rate, its sound tracks with their languages, whether it is scrambled and whether a channel already takes it. Tick several and add them in one go with one profile. The scan reads the feed through the same input a channel uses.
* Transcode profiles: five built in (pass through, sound to AAC, and 480p, 720p and 1080p H.264) and any number saved on the station, each with codec, size and fit, bitrate, keyframes, deinterlacing, sound codec, bitrate and tracks, and the encoder on Auto, the GPU or the CPU. Changing a profile changes every channel that takes it.
* The station keeps the channels and profiles in `headend.json` and runs each channel as the direct show a Flussonic import makes, so the tiles, health and the Flussonic compatible URLs work the same for both. A Flussonic import now makes channels the station keeps, which are edited like any other.
* With a Flussonic file followed, editing or removing one of its streams on the screen takes that stream out of the file's hands, and the screen says so before saving. Follow the file again gives it back.
* `godwinmix headend` runs with no `--flussonic`, and with `--port` answers Flussonic's paths and API for the station's own channels.
* A rendition can keep its encoder on the GPU or on the CPU (`video.encoder`).
* New station methods: `headend.channel.list`, `add`, `update`, `bulk`, `remove` and `follow`, `headend.profile.save` and `remove`, `headend.scan` and `headend.export`, each an agent tool behind `search_tools`. See [Set up a headend in GodwinMix](docs/how-to/set-up-a-headend.md) and [the reference](docs/reference/headend-channels.md).

Channels split across two or more NVIDIA cards.

* A rendition names the card its conversion runs on (`video.gpu`, a CUDA device index, or `auto`). The decode, the scale and the encode all run on that card, by that card's own decoder and encoder (`nvh264device1enc` on the second) and in its memory, with one CUDA context per card shared by every channel on it.
* `auto` puts a channel on the card with the least work, by what the governor holds on each, counting channels whose input has not arrived yet, so fifty that start at once are dealt out evenly. A placed channel stays on its card while it runs, since a move restarts its encode. `gpu.rebalance` (and Even out the cards on the headend screen) moves the fewest channels from the busiest card to the idlest, with a dry run first. `[hardware] gpus = "spread"` makes `auto` the default for renditions that name no card; it is `first` unless set.
* A Flussonic `transcoder deviceid=N` is no longer ignored. It is read as `auto` by default (`[headend] flussonic_deviceid = "spread"`), because Flussonic writes `deviceid=0` on every stream and taking it literally would leave the second card idle; `flussonic_deviceid = "file"` takes it as written. The import dialog offers the choice, spreading first, on a machine with several cards. `headend.export` writes `deviceid` for a channel set on a card, and the API keeps echoing the file's.
* The governor counts each card apart (`nvidia` is card 0, as calibrations have always named it, then `nvidia:1`), applies `nvenc_sessions` and the calibrated session limit to every card, and `governor.status` lists them all. `gpu.status` from a station says which channels convert on each card.
* On the headend screen: a GPU field in the channel editor (Auto, GPU 0, GPU 1), Spread across GPUs and Move to GPU for the ticked channels, the card each channel is on in the channel list, and the channels on each card in the GPU strip. `headend.channel.bulk` takes `gpu`, `headend.channel.list` answers `gpus` and each channel's `on_gpu`, and the agent tool `rebalance_gpus` is behind `search_tools`. See [Split channels across GPUs](docs/how-to/split-channels-across-gpus.md).
* A direct show removed and made again before the station's next table, which is how a headend remakes a channel whose conversion changed, kept nothing of what its input had said, and waited for ever although the input was live: of six channels remade at once, one came back. The station now forgets a removed show only once the table it hands the host no longer has it.
* A headend that followed a Flussonic file under an earlier version keeps following it on the same folder. Its channels keep their show ids, outputs and Flussonic paths; each is made again once on the first start, because its rendition now carries `gpu`.

## 0.3.17 (2026-10-24)

Headend mode: GodwinMix runs a box of off air channels on one GPU in place of Flussonic, reading Flussonic's config and answering its URLs and API, so the gateways, caches, players and monitoring around it keep working. A new flow map shows any station's inputs, shows, conversions and outputs live.

* `godwinmix headend --flussonic <file>` reads a Flussonic config as it is and keeps following it: each `stream` becomes a direct show, a `transcoder` line becomes its rendition, and a change to the file is applied within a second. The facade answers `/<name>/index.m3u8`, the `tracks-*` playlists, TS segments named by their UTC start, `preview.jpg`, `embed.html` and `/streamer/api/v3/streams` with Basic auth from `edit_auth`, and writes Flussonic style log lines for scrambled inputs and refused encoder sessions. See [Replace Flussonic](docs/how-to/replace-flussonic.md) and [the facade reference](docs/reference/flussonic-facade.md).
* MPTS multicast is read by a native Rust input: one socket and one thread per group, shared by every programme taken from it. MPEG-2 video and every sound track are carried, each track with its language from the PMT.
* Conversion stays on the GPU from decode to encode: NVDEC for MPEG-2, H.264 and HEVC, scale and pad in CUDA memory, NVENC, with CPU fallbacks for each step. A rendition takes fit (pad with a colour, crop, stretch), deinterlace, a keyframe interval in frames, preset, B frames and every sound track. The governor's NVENC session probe goes past 8 when the driver allows it. On a box with an RTX 3060 class card, 46 channels converted at once with sound and picture within a frame of each other.
* HLS outputs carry every sound track as an `EXT-X-MEDIA` rendition, make MPEG-TS segments beside CMAF (`segments` is `cmaf`, `ts` or `both`), and take `access: open` for an output behind a gateway that checks its own signature.
* The headend view (View > Headend) shows every channel as a live tile with its state and sound levels, the GPU's encoder, decoder, memory and sessions, the input groups and the machine. `gpu.status` and `machine.status` are new station methods. See [Watch a headend](docs/how-to/watch-a-headend.md).
* The flow map (View > Flow map) draws what comes in, how it is connected and what goes out, for a mixed show and for fifty channels alike. Dots move along each link at a pace set by its bit rate, a dead link goes grey, and hovering anything shows its details and lights its whole path. It held 55 to 60 frames a second with 200 channels. See [Read the flow map](docs/how-to/read-the-flow-map.md).
* Every headend setting is in the UI, the rarely used ones under a closed Advanced section: an HLS output's segments, access, segment length and window, a rendition's fit, deinterlace, keyframes, preset, B frames and sound, a Flussonic import with its report before anything is made (File > Import a Flussonic config), and the NVENC session limits in Mixer settings. The `headend` agent profile gains `gpu_status` and `machine_status`, and `import_flussonic` and `headend_status` are found through `search_tools`.
* A station's page works when no mixer is running, so a headend station's page opens on the headend view instead of saying Disconnected.
* Converters decode H.264 with libav before openh264, which refuses 4:4:4, 4:2:2, 10 bit and interlaced streams. A rendition with no frame rate asked for keeps the rate the source declares. The crop fit links at the source's own rate. An `rtmp://` pull uses librtmp where it is installed, which plays from `ffmpeg -listen` and MediaMTX where rtmp2src did not.
* Clicking another show's tab switches to it again.

Fixed from a test of an early 0.3.17 build on a Windows 11 desktop:

* After an upgrade that moved the app to another folder (the old setup.exe installed under the user's folder, the .msi installs in Program Files), the plugins it had installed on first use, such as ingest and NDI, stayed at the old version, because the app took them for the operator's own. A copy marked as shipped with GodwinMix is now brought up to date wherever the app that installed it lived. A plugin the operator installed is still never touched.
* With the monitoring wall, the headend page, the flow map, routing or the plugins page open, a click on a show tab left that page covering the show, so a switch looked like it did nothing, and on the show's own tab it opened the tab's menu or a rename. A click on any tab now closes the page that covers the show; on another show's tab it also switches, and on the show's own tab it does nothing more.
* View > Headend did nothing at all. Its command was in the menu but never registered, so the click ran nothing. It opens the headend view now, on any station.
* A direct show's HLS output could sit in Reconnecting for ever with "nothing came from the relay for 30 s". The ingest plugin took 127.0.0.1 on its RTMP port as the relay without checking that it reached the plugin, and on Windows (macOS too) another program can hold that port on loopback beside it: a GodwinMix or an ingest plugin left running from an earlier start, which answered with nothing. The plugin now asks that address which server it reached, and when it is not its own, or the RTMP port is taken, it opens the relay on a loopback port of its own and says so in the log. Every direct output's state and reason are now written to the log when they change, and a wrong path under `/hls/<output>/` names the playlists that work.
* HLS for viewers with a ladder was refused with "needs 0.0 cores and 0.0 cores is free" on a machine 40 to 50% busy. The governor counted a spike from another program twice, once in that program's peak and again in the reserve, which on a desktop with a browser open could add up to more than the machine. It is counted once now, and a refusal of under a tenth of a core says it in hundredths.
* Each custom rung in the HLS dialog has an Advanced group (fit, bar colour, deinterlace, preset, B frames), and the ladder has one sound bitrate for every rung. A rung's keyframes stay on the segment length, so every rung's segments line up.
* An output can be edited after it is saved: Edit on an HLS row of the Outputs panel opens the HLS dialog filled in, an output to a plain address (`udp://239.10.0.1:5001`) edits as an address, and each output in a direct show's detail has Edit. A direct show's tab menu has Outputs and settings.
* The flow map's hover card for a link no longer ends in a line reading null, and a mixer's own HLS output is drawn as HLS, not RTMP.
* Sources > Test pattern and the other kinds put the new source into the scene on screen, as the tray's own Add does.
* In the desktop app on Windows, a maximized window that leaves full screen (the flow map's, for one) is maximized again instead of reaching under the taskbar.
* In the desktop app on Windows and Linux, Window > Toggle Full Screen and F11 did nothing at all, from maximized or restored. The menu used the system's own full screen item, which only macOS has. The app now has its own there, with F11, and the menu bar is hidden while the window is full screen.
* The desktop app put its window back at the size it was saved with, even when that no longer fit: a size saved by 0.3.16 on a 2880 by 1800 laptop opened the window under the taskbar and past the bottom of the screen. A window put back bigger than its screen's work area, or partly off it, is now moved and shrunk to fit before it is maximized again.
* A plugin the operator installed by hand (`gmx plugin add`) is still kept in place of the one the app carries, but no longer silently: an older ingest kept that way made an HLS output fail with "nothing came from the relay for 30 s". At each launch the desktop app writes a line to `mixer.log` for each such copy, with its version and folder, saying the bundled copy is not used and that moving the folder out of the plugins folder uses it. The plugins list in the UI does not show this yet.
* A direct show's tab said "black" and "No destinations", and its Outputs panel "Nothing is being sent anywhere", while its HLS output was live. A direct show has no mixer behind its page, so its outputs are now read from the station: the header counts them, the tally shows the input, and the Outputs panel lists them with a way into the show's outputs and settings.
* A direct show's outputs follow its input. After the input stopped, the headend's output table still showed an HLS output as live at 2.1 Mb/s; it now says waiting, because the input is not live, and counts no rate.
* A direct show's HLS output shows its CMAF and MPEG-TS playlist links (`/hls/<output>/master.m3u8` and `master-ts.m3u8`, with the show and the viewer key), each with Copy, in its row of the show's outputs. A playlist the output does not make says so and where to turn it on.
* Preview no longer calls a scene item whose source was removed "not in this mixer" beside "is not running": it says the source was removed and is left out of the take, and the note says it is no longer in this mixer.
* The "Still streaming from before" banner came back over the show tabs after every tab switch until Keep was pressed again. Once answered with Keep or Stop all it stays away for the same outputs for the rest of the session, and comes back only for something new.
* Show tabs switch the way a browser's tabs do. One click on another show's tab marks it chosen at once and moves the page to that show; a click used to wait a quarter second for a possible second click, and a double click or double tap on another show's tab opened its name for renaming and never switched. Double click renames only the show you are on now. `Ctrl+Tab` and `Ctrl+Shift+Tab` (and `Ctrl+PageDown`, `Ctrl+PageUp`, `Cmd+Shift+]` and `Cmd+Shift+[` on a Mac) go to the next show and the one before. A redraw of the tabs no longer drops the keyboard focus off the tab it was on. See [Run several shows](docs/how-to/run-several-shows.md).
* A show tab closes the way a browser's tab does: a `×` on the tab you are on and on any tab under the pointer (always, under a finger or in a narrow window), or a middle click, and neither switches to the tab first. Closing is what quitting the desktop app is to the machine: nothing is deleted, and the show's scenes, sources and outputs come back when it is opened again. With nothing live it closes at once; when it is on air, streaming, serving a watch link, recording or taking an encoder, the page lists what is running and offers Stop it and close, Close and keep it running, or Cancel, which has the keyboard. The station keeps which shows are closed (`show.close` with `keep_running`, `show.open`, and `closed` in `show.list`), across browsers and restarts: a closed show does not start with the station unless it was kept running. File, Switch show lists closed shows, File, Reopen closed show (`Ctrl+Shift+T` in the desktop app) brings back the one closed last, and the monitoring wall shows them with an Open button. The last open tab's `×` is the desktop app's own Quit; in a browser it says the station keeps running. Remove show is still the one that deletes, from the tab's menu, after its question. The page a volunteer opens grew by 1087 bytes for the `×`, its styles and the key, and its budget by 1 kB to 398 kB. See [Run several shows](docs/how-to/run-several-shows.md#close-a-show).
* `dev/windows-firewall.ps1` also removes the Block rules Windows writes when a firewall prompt is cancelled. A Block rule wins over every Allow rule, the installer's included, so one cancelled prompt kept a source on the local network from ever connecting. It allows the app's plugin copies and any test folder given with `-Path`, keeps its rules for builds that still exist, and `-List` shows what it would change.

## 0.3.16 (2026-10-21)

From tester issue 24: Interact shows a web page sharp enough to read, so YouTube's quality can be picked there. And a page with a looping clip can no longer fill the disk.

* Interact showed a web page source as its cell of the multiview, a fraction of a 1920 pixel sheet stretched to the dialog, so the page's text and a video player's menus were too blurred to read. It now shows the page at the canvas size, made from the source itself only while Interact is open, and it works with the multiview switched off. On the tester's YouTube page (a live news stream) the gear menu, Quality and 1080p were all readable and clickable, and the menu then read Quality 1080p HD; YouTube had picked Auto (720p) on its own. See [Use a web page by hand](docs/how-to/use-a-web-page-by-hand.md).
* `/mjpeg/{source}?full=1` is that picture for any client: one source at the canvas size, off the source's own tee, behind a leaky queue so it cannot slow the programme. `width` and `fps` cap it. It is counted under `gmx_stream_clients{kind="source"}`. See [Streams](docs/reference/streams.md).
* A web page with a video on it (a superimposed source) fetched its clip to the temp folder on every build of the source, and a copy made for a build that never ran was never deleted. A box whose source was rebuilt every few minutes filled a 915 GB disk with 18,820 copies of one 44 MB clip. Every build of the same clip now shares one copy, deleted when the last one lets go, and before its first fetch a process deletes the copies an earlier run left behind (a crash, a kill, a container restart). Only files named the way a copy is named are touched.
* Two tests that failed now and then for reasons outside the code they test: the RTSP output's `uridecodebin` test asked for video alone, so the decoded sound counted as an unknown type and ended the player with nothing decoded about one run in fifteen on macOS; it now takes sound and picture as the core does, and passed 40 runs of 40 there. The restart leak test now waits for the last restart's worker to let go instead of a fixed second.

## 0.3.15 (2026-10-19)

Linux arm64 joins the other five platforms on our own CEF, so the browser renderer built on a Raspberry Pi or another arm64 Linux machine plays H.264 and AAC too. The release has no arm64 Linux desktop download yet, so this reaches you when you build from a checkout.

* The Linux arm64 build of CEF 154.0.34 with H.264 and AAC is published in [psmux/godwinmix-cef](https://github.com/psmux/godwinmix-cef/releases/tag/cef-154.0.34), next to the Windows, Windows arm64, Linux and both macOS builds, and a checkout fetches it from there with its checksums. ## 0.3.5 (2026-10-12)

* A show on Linux keeps its memory level through a long session. In the nightly hour long soak the show process grew from 475 to 527 MB, more slowly as the hour went on, and nothing in it was being kept: every pipeline had the same elements and pads round after round, and GStreamer's leak tracer found no object left over between checkpoints. It was glibc giving the threads of each mosaic and audio branch that came and went their own malloc arenas, up to eight per core, and keeping each arena's high water mark. * Opening a PCM or Opus monitor on the programme no longer stops the programme's sound. The monitoring branch was linked to the audio tee before it was started, and a buffer that landed in the few microseconds between found its queue not yet active. The tee passed that refusal back to the audio mixer, which paused and stayed paused until something happened to restart it. In the nightly soak that was about one open in 540, and the client waited twenty seconds for nothing; in one run without the sources phase the sound never came back. The branch is now started before it is linked, as the thumbnail branches already were, and its sink no longer waits for a preroll. A local raw preview on Linux and macOS had the same order on the video tee and has the same fix.
* New metric `gmx_programme_frame_gap_ms`, the longest gap between two programme frames in the last 30 to 60 seconds. Over two frame periods means a whole frame time passed with no frame. See `docs/reference/metrics.md`.
* `dev/soak.sh` judges a stall by that gap against three frame periods (100 ms at 30 fps), from the warm up sample on, instead of by the sixty frame average against 34 ms, which a hosted runner went over with nothing wrong. It waits for the station's first run encoder calibration before its first round, and still records the average.
* A direct show's rendition keeps its frame rate when the input is read again from the start or its sender restarts. The second around the restart read as 1 or 3 fps, the station took that reading in place of the 30 fps it had measured, and the rendition was planned again at 1 fps and sat waiting. The measured rate is now kept while the picture keeps its codec and size.
* Two devices that open the page at the same moment see each other in the presence list. One that joined while the other's subscribe answer was being written was announced to nobody, and the first device never listed it until somebody else came or went.
* A camera whose first frames arrive late and drain slowly, as a camera plugin's frames over a pipe do on a busy Windows machine, is caught up by the whole of its delay. The catch up guard moved it while the backlog was still arriving, by as little as 0.2 of 1.5 seconds, then read the rest of the backlog as a source that fills up again and left it a second behind for good. It now waits until the delay stops growing.
* GodwinMix runs without gst-libav, and uses FFmpeg's code only for a codec nothing else on the machine handles. The programme's AAC is encoded by `fdkaacenc` where it is installed, Apple's `atenc` on a Mac, Windows' `mfaacenc`, then `voaacenc`, and `avenc_aac` only when none of those exists. Sources are decoded by `openh264dec` for H.264 after the hardware decoders, `libde265dec` or `d3d11h265dec` for HEVC, `fdkaacdec`, `mfaacdec`, `faad` or Apple's `atdec` for AAC, and `a52dec` for AC-3, each ahead of its libav decoder. The mixer swaps GStreamer ranks at start so `decodebin` follows the same order when it opens a source, without moving any hardware decoder. See `docs/how-to/run-without-ffmpeg.md` and `docs/explanation/ffmpeg.md`.
* The A/V offset follows the new AAC encoders. `voaacenc` was assumed to delay its sound 21 ms like `avenc_aac`; it is 33 ms. `mfaacenc` takes its delay out itself and needs none, and `atenc` leaves Apple's 44 ms in. A new test measures every installed AAC encoder through every installed AAC decoder with a beep at a known time, on each CI platform, and fails when `priming_delay_ms` is off by more than 4 ms.
* `gmx doctor` says which AAC encoder the programme gets from the catalogue (a warning when it is `avenc_aac`), whether gst-libav is installed, what it is used for, and which codecs nothing on the machine decodes.
* A node's AAC encoder and a WHIP publisher's Opus to AAC conversion take the best AAC encoder installed instead of naming `avenc_aac`. `gmx codec test` on an entry that only encodes, such as `aac-vo`, decodes with the best software decoder installed for its codec.
* The desktop `.deb` recommends gstreamer1.0-libav instead of depending on it. The Docker image has `--build-arg WITH_LIBAV=0` for an image with no FFmpeg, and keeps `a52dec` beside `x264enc` from the ugly set. The bundled runtimes' checks ask for any H.264 decoder and any AAC decoder and encoder and say which they found, where they asked for libav's by name.
* A new CI job on Linux uninstalls gst-libav, then records a programme and decodes it back, runs the catalogue and doctor tests and the smoke test, and fails if anything reached for libav.
* Still only in libav: E-AC-3, ProRes and QuickTime Animation clips with alpha, and software HEVC on Windows and macOS. The macOS app, trimmed from Homebrew, has no openh264, no voaacenc and no a52dec, so on a Mac AC-3 needs libav too.

* A rendition planner, the new crate `godwinmix-render`. Given what every output asks for (container, codec, size, frame rate, bitrate, keyframe interval, audio) and what every source carries, `plan` returns the smallest graph that serves them all: a copy when the source already matches, one decode per source, one scale per distinct size and rate, one encoder per distinct shape shared by every output that wants it, and one keyframe interval across every rendition of a source so a ladder switches cleanly. * A resource governor, `crates/godwinmix-govern`, not wired into the mixer yet. It answers one question before anything starts: can this machine do this without taking a frame from what is on air? `Governor::admit(cost, what)` grants a ticket that holds a share of CPU, hardware encoder load and sessions, memory and uplink until it is dropped, or refuses with what the work needs, what is free and which renditions would fit (`1080p30 H.264 on h264-software-x264 fits, or 1080p60 H.264 on the GPU encoder h264-gpu`). What is free is worked out, not set: live load sampled once a second (`/proc` on Linux, Mach host statistics on macOS, `GetSystemTimes` on Windows), other programs counted at their recent peak, and a reserve sized for a desktop or a headless server, with an optional `reserve_cores` override. `shed()` says what to give up when the machine runs short on air: thumbnails and previews, then the lowest ladder rung upward, then faster software presets, never the programme or a live output's top rung. * A new first party plugin, `udp`, for MPEG-TS over UDP and RTP. `udp/source` receives a unicast port or a multicast group (`udp://@239.1.1.1:5000`, source specific as `udp://10.0.0.9@232.1.1.1:5000`), on the interface you name, and takes one program out of a multiplex by number or by PID, with the PAT and PMT rewritten to match. * A frame bus, the new crate `godwinmix-framebus`: one owner decodes a device or a stream once and any number of readers, in the same process or in others, read the decoded frames from shared memory with no copy. A reader that falls behind skips to the newest frame and never slows the owner or another reader; the owner never waits on a reader and drops a frame, counted, only if every slot is leased; a reader killed mid read leaves nothing locked, because the owner frees its leases when its socket closes; a reader keeps its frames when the owner dies and attaches to the next one by itself. A destination waits while its stream is not live, and one set to send whichever stream is live moves to the next when that one leaves. Destinations are kept with their channel in `<stem>.runtime.channels.toml`, their addresses and keys sealed in the secret store beside the channel's keys, and come back after a restart. On one channel with two live 720p streams and three destinations, the channel server process measured about 1.2% of one core and 13 MB on an M4 Pro.
* The Channels page reads `channel.list` every two seconds while it is on screen and something is live, so a stream's bit rate and its sparkline move, and a destination that is trying again says why on its tile.
* The core takes every platform the page offers (Instagram Live, Kick, LinkedIn Live, X and TikTok LIVE as well as YouTube, Facebook, Twitch, custom and SRT); a test reads the page's table and fails when one is missing.
* `channel.key.add` and `channel.key.remove` are `POST /api/v1/channels/{id}/key/add` and `.../key/remove`, and the destination methods are `POST /api/v1/channels/{id}/destination/add`, `POST /api/v1/channels/{id}/destination` for set and `.../destination/remove`. The three destination methods had shared one path. A test now fails when two methods land on one route.
* A Channels tab for RTMP channels, next to Outputs, loaded the first time it is shown. Three new methods, admin scope: `channel.destination.add`, `channel.destination.set` and `channel.destination.remove`. Keys are write only. Each destination has its own threads and a bounded queue that drops whole GOPs when the far end is slow, reconnects on the same own and cdn backoff outputs use, gives the far end the metadata and codec headers again on every connection and then waits for a keyframe, and reports `state`, `kbps`, `reconnects` and an `error` in words. The platform servers and the two retry presets now live in the protocol crate, and the core's outputs read the presets from there.
* RTMP channels, the server half. A channel is a named place encoders publish to on the mixer's one RTMP port, `rtmp://<mixer>:1935/<app>/<stream>?psk=<key>`, and several streams can be live on one channel at once. Seven new methods: `channel.list`, `channel.get`, `channel.add`, `channel.set`, `channel.remove`, `channel.key.add` and `channel.key.remove`, and three events, `channel.changed`, `channel.removed` and `channel.refused`. A key rides as `?psk=`, `?key=`, `?token=` or `?Token=`, or is the whole stream name on a channel set that way; it is shown once when made and sealed in the secret store after that, with only its last four characters read back. A publisher with no key or a wrong one is refused with `NetStream.Publish.Denied` and a sentence, logged, and announced. Taking a key back or switching a channel off cuts off whoever is on air with it. A stream that goes live becomes a source called `<app>-<stream>` by itself, and goes again when the publisher leaves unless a scene holds it, in which case it waits on its freeze frame and comes back with the publisher. Each live stream reports its codec, size, frame rate and bit rate. Channels are kept in `<stem>.runtime.channels.toml` beside the runtime store and survive a restart. See `docs/reference/channels.md` and `docs/how-to/rtmp-channels.md`.
* The ingest plugin's `ingest/discover` is the channel server. Every stream goes through a hub where each reader has a bounded queue: a reader that falls behind loses whole GOPs from the front, counted as `dropped_gops`, and never slows the publisher or the other readers. A tag's payload is shared between readers, never copied per reader. A late reader gets `onMetaData` and the codec headers before its first keyframe. A mixer source reads its stream over loopback on the RTMP port itself, where it used to get a loopback port of its own per publisher; the old relay also wrote every publisher's bytes to every relay, and blocked the publisher on a slow reader. New `streams` tool. Eight 1080p30 6 Mbit/s publishers on three channels with two readers each cost the channel server about 4% of one core.

## 0.2.0 (2026-09-15)

The first GodwinMix release, grown from LiveboxMix 0.1.0.

* The core is a library (`godwinmix-core`) with a versioned protocol (`godwinmix-protocol`, api level 1) served over `/rpc` and `/api/v1`, with generated TypeScript, Python and Rust clients.
* Plugins are processes on a documented contract: a manifest, JSON lines on stdio, media over unixfd, shm or a pipe. Twelve first party plugins, an SDK, templates in five languages, a conformance harness, marketplaces with signed installs, and a WASM tier behind a feature.
* Scenes as a document the core owns, a slot pool on the compositor, transitions on control bindings, a composer in the web UI, layouts, presets, themes, an OBS importer and OGraf graphics.
* Nothing runs unless asked: the multiview, thumbnails, preview, audio monitoring and the encoder start with the first client and stop with the last. Idle core measured at 0.012 cores and 37 MB on an M4 Pro.
* Safety in the core, a compact agent surface with MCP over stdio and HTTP, hooks, session replay and an operator eval suite.
* A terminal UI, Companion, Stream Deck, OSC and tally integrations, a node daemon for remote placements over mTLS.
* Desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux with a trimmed GStreamer bundled; Docker image and a headless quickstart.

Known limits: Linux and Windows builds are verified by inspection and cross checks only until the project's CI runs; browser sources on macOS and Windows need the codec enabled CEF the project publishes; the second canvas is not built.
