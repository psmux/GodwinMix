# Changelog

## Unreleased

* A direct show follows a sender that restarts. An encoder stopped and started again on the same UDP port with new PIDs, a new program number or a stream added to its PMT left the input saying `a second video stream (video/x-h264); the first is taken` with no frames, for good: `tsdemux` offers the new streams before it removes the old ones, and the input took the first stream of each kind and sent every later one to a fakesink. Every stream now gets its chain behind a probe, the one carried passes and the others wait, and when the carried stream's pad goes the first waiting stream of its kind takes over; a second video stream in the same live program is still left out and named. A jump in the input's clock that the demuxer does not smooth over is laid after the last tag, as a reconnect is. The station also took the one second reading that straddles a restart, a frame or two, for the feed's frame rate and planned its renditions again at 1 fps; it now needs two readings in a row that agree. Tested with gst-launch, ffmpeg and SRT senders restarted with new and with the same layouts in `plugins/ingest/src/direct/input/tests/restart.rs`, and through a station in `crates/godwinmix/tests/station/direct_restart.rs`, where a UDP copy and a transcoded UDP output were sending again about 600 ms after each new sender started. See `docs/how-to/headend-feeds.md#when-the-sender-restarts`.
* A show that composites raises no `silence` with its alarms off, and none with nothing to be silent. The station's own show `main`, which has no source, alarmed `silence` on every fresh station whatever `[vitals] alarms` said. `[vitals] alarms` (and `enabled` in `show.set {alarms}`) now switches black, freeze and silence on and off for a show that composites, as `monitor.alarms` does for a direct show, and silence is judged only while the mixer hears a live source with sound on programme. Tested in `crates/godwinmix-core/src/vitals/tests.rs` on a real programme and against a real station in `crates/godwinmix/tests/station/health_main.rs`: no alarm on `main` with alarms off or with no source, and `silence` once a muted tone is on programme with alarms on. See `docs/reference/show-health.md`.
* A show that mixes has a current picture on the monitoring wall, the same size and cadence a direct show's has. The station used to ask such a show for the programme cell of its mosaic, which built a whole mosaic for one small picture, waited on it from cold, and was refused two asks in three by the snapshot rate limit at the wall's two second cadence. A show now answers the new `program.thumbnail {width?}` (`GET /api/v1/program/thumbnail`) in the direct host's shape, `{jpeg, width, height, at_ms}` or `{pending: true}`, from a branch on its raw programme tee behind a leaky queue of its own: one frame a second, scaled to 320 wide, for ten seconds after an ask, then taken off the tee, with `gmx_stream_clients{kind="thumbnail"}` 1 while it runs. The station reads it over the show's public REST route and serves it at the same `/api/v1/shows/{id}/thumbnail.jpg`. A show that is stopped, failed or starting answers 409 with its state, and the wall says so on the frame instead of keeping the last picture. Tested against a real station with a moving test source in `crates/godwinmix/tests/station/wall.rs`, and on the software and GL graphics entries in the core. See `docs/reference/show-health.md#thumbnails`.
* A show without compositing can serve HLS: `show.output.add {id, uri: "hls://viewers", params?}` packages the feed with the same packager, routes, viewer keys, LL-HLS and DASH a show's `hls/output` has, at `/hls/viewers/master.m3u8?show=<id>&key=...` on the station's port (the output's `playback` in `show.list`). The packaging runs in the HLS packager, the station's binary run as a child with `--hls-packager` only while such an output is on; the station checks the viewer key and forwards the request on loopback. A crash there costs those outputs a few seconds: they say `reconnecting` (`failed` after five stops in a row), the station starts the packager again, and they come back on the same link, while the control port and every other output carry on. The input's H.264, HEVC or AV1 and AAC are copied, not decoded, and every segment starts on one of the feed's keyframes. Sound HLS does not carry (MP2, AC-3) is refused with the rendition that fixes it, `{"audio": {"codec": "aac"}}`, and that rendition now works: MPEG audio and AC-3 are decoded for a rendition, where before the transcoder gave them AAC caps and the planner refused them. Tested with real UDP feeds through a station, H.264 and AAC copied and H.264 and MP2 converted, each segment checked with `gst-discoverer-1.0`, in `crates/godwinmix/tests/station/hls_direct.rs`, and the packager killed with SIGKILL beside a second direct show in `hls_isolation.rs`; HEVC was checked by hand with `ffprobe`, and AV1 not at all.
* A direct show's silence alarm now listens to MPEG layer II, MP3, AC-3 and E-AC-3 sound as well as AAC. The vitals decoded every burst of sound with the AAC header, so those codecs were never measured and a feed that really went quiet raised nothing. Each burst is now decoded as its own frames say it is. The one `silence` alarm in the 200 feed direct run was on the station's own compositing show `main`, which has no source and is silent; no MPEG audio feed alarmed. Tested with ffmpeg feeds over UDP in `plugins/ingest/src/direct/vitals/tests_codecs.rs`.
* The wall's Load column said copy only for every show that mixes, because it looked only for an output with an encoder and a show that mixes has none in `show.stats`; `show.stats` now says what each show does (`work`: `mix`, `transcode` or `copy`) and what it costs (`cpu_millicores`, read off the process for a show that mixes), and the column reads them. The header's CPU left out the show processes that hold no ticket and the ingest plugin that runs the direct shows, so 200 direct shows taking five cores read as 0%; a station's `governor.status` now answers `cpu.measured_millicores`, every process the station started read when it is called, and the header shows that.
* Four faults in direct shows, each reproduced on a release station and fixed. The ingest plugin installed while a station ran was started by the show that answered `plugin.add`, in that show's process and without the direct table, so every direct show sat in `no-input` with nothing listening until the station restarted; a show now leaves the ingest plugin to the station, and the station starts it itself when a `plugin.add`, `plugin.update`, `plugin.enable` or `plugin.reload` passes through, looking once a second until it runs. The station could also start the plugin before its first direct table was built, and a table handed while the plugin was still starting reached nobody, so after a restart, or with a show added in the plugin's first second, the shows had no input and their outputs said `waiting` for good; the station now waits for the first table before starting the plugin and hands it again once it runs. An alarm the station adds itself (`no-input`, `output-failed`, `governor-refused`) took a new `since_ms` on every read, and a `cc-errors` or `loss` alarm's start slid with its window, so the wall showed every alarm's age as nothing; each alarm now keeps the time it began until it clears. And `show.add_many` judged renditions against the CPU's room alone, which has no room on any encoding device, so a hardware encode was always refused; `have` now carries the encoding device's room, and a station asked in its first second waits for its first reading of the load. A new test runs a real UDP feed through a direct show to a UDP copy output with the ingest plugin installed while the station runs, then again after a restart (`crates/godwinmix/tests/station/direct_live.rs`). The scale harness installs the ingest plugin in direct mode, keeps its plugins and secrets in the run's folder through `plugins_dir` and `GODWINMIX_HOME`, and counts `gmx-ingest` as the direct host. On an M4 Pro, 200 unicast UDP feeds at 1.025 Gbit/s into 200 direct shows, each copied to a UDP output, ran clean for the measured minute: every output arrived, no continuity error, no GOP dropped, the longest silence 112 ms, with the direct host at about 5.2 cores and 205 MiB and the station under 1% of one core; see `dev/bench/results/scale-m4pro-2026-10-01-1614-direct.md`.
* `gmx plugin add` against a running station no longer fails while it waits for the install with "duplicate field `task_id`". `gmx` reads a task by its path, `GET /api/v1/tasks/{id}`, as a plain REST client does, and `task.get` and `task.cancel` take the id as `task_id`, as `id` from the path, or both when they agree; two that disagree are refused by name. Tested against a real station in `crates/godwinmix/tests/station/home.rs`.
* `GODWINMIX_HOME` moves every per user path, for the core, the station, its shows and `gmx` alike: plugins, the secret store, the frame bus registry, saved presets, the marketplace list and the installed codec catalogue. Plugins used to be found from `HOME` whatever it said, so a scratch `GODWINMIX_HOME` installed into the real `~/.godwinmix/plugins`. `[control] plugins_dir` and `GODWINMIX_PLUGINS_DIR` still win for the plugins folder, and a `plugins_dir` starting with `~/` is read against the home folder. The paths are worked out in one place, `godwinmix_host::home`, and listed in `docs/reference/configuration.md`. A test runs a station and `gmx plugin add` with `HOME` pointed at an empty folder and checks it stays empty.
* A switch of compositing with `show.set` answers at once with a `task_id` instead of holding the call for up to 30 seconds when the show's outputs do not come live, which they never do with no input flowing. The station answers `task.get` for these tasks itself, and the task's result is the answer `show.set` used to give, with the `switch` report. A refusal to switch off still comes back on the call, a second switch of the same show while one runs is refused with the task to wait for, and a switch cannot be cancelled halfway. `gmx shows set --compositing` waits for the task and prints the result, and the monitoring wall's switch says Switching on the row until it lands. See `docs/reference/shows.md`.
* An agent can run a headend. The station's methods are MCP tools now, bound from one table in the protocol crate (`mcp_bindings`) so a binding is written once and takes effect when its method is registered: `add_shows` (`show.add_many`), `show_stats`, `set_show`, `set_show_output`, `add_show`, `remove_show`, `remove_shows`, `start_show`, `stop_show`, `rename_show`, `add_show_output`, `remove_show_output`, `governor_status`, `calibrate_governor`, `plan_rendition`, `export_project`, `import_project`, `add_channel`, `get_channel`, `set_channel`, `remove_channel` and the three channel destination tools. The standard profile's eleven hot tools are chosen again with shows first: `list_shows`, `add_shows`, `show_stats`, `set_show`, `set_show_output`, then `agent_state`, `take`, `revert`, `go_live`, `add_source` and `list_sources`, with `search_tools`. `status`, `snapshot`, `remove_source`, `list_outputs` and `add_output` moved behind `search_tools`; `minimal` is unchanged. A tool that one show answers takes `show: <id>` in the standard profile, sent as `?show=<id>` so the station passes it to that show. Measured by `crates/godwinmix/tests/agent_headend`, which drives `gmx mcp` over stdio against a real station: twenty UDP feeds added with a dry run and the apply, and read back with one `show_stats`, in five tool calls and about two seconds, 9.3 kB read for the work after a 13.2 kB tool list. A tool answer over 2 kB is now sent compact rather than indented, which roughly halves what a large `show_stats` costs. See `docs/how-to/use-with-an-ai-agent.md` and `skills/godwinmix-operate/SKILL.md`.
* `gmx shows`: `list`, `add --from feeds.csv [--dry-run] [--compositing on]` (one `show.add_many` for the whole list, a CSV with a `name,input,program,outputs,format` header or one address per line, the list `gmx-scale feeds` writes included), `stats [--watch]` (one line per show, worst first, with alarms and their age) and `set <id> --compositing on|off`. See `docs/reference/cli.md`.
* A show that composites now reaches the monitoring wall with its health. The show sends what its vitals judged over the station link (`show.health {health}`, beside `show.on_air` and `show.load`) when its state or set of alarm kinds changes, and the station keeps it per show, adds its input's alarms, sends `event/show.health {id, health}` and serves it in `show.list` and `show.stats` as it does for a direct show. A show whose process dies, is killed or loses its link reads as `alarm` with one `stall` alarm from the moment the link closed until a new process has sent its first health, never as the last health the old one sent; one stopped on purpose reads `off` without an alarm on the way. The wall's alarm settings reach such a show too: the station calls the show's `vitals.set` with `show.set {alarms}` translated (`*_ms` to `*_secs`, `silence_dbfs` to `silence_db`, `enabled` to `alarms`) whenever the show links and whenever the settings change while it runs. See `docs/reference/show-health.md` and `docs/reference/shows.md`.
* An SRT passphrase in a show's input, written in the address (`srt://...?passphrase=...`) or in `params`, for the input or its backup, is sealed in the secret store under `show.<id>.input` and no longer stored in `shows.json` in the clear. The list and `show.list` carry the sentinel `__secret__` in its place; an input sent back with the sentinel keeps the sealed passphrase, and only the direct host's table holds the real one. A list written before this is sealed when the station starts. See `docs/reference/shows.md#input-passphrases`.
* Show health: a thumbnail and alarms for every show, cheap enough for 200 at once. A show with compositing off is watched by the direct host's vitals (`plugins/ingest/src/direct/vitals/`), which decode keyframes alone, at most one a second, on two worker threads shared by every show with the machine's hardware decoder when it has one, and three AAC frames a second for the silence check. They raise `black`, `freeze`, `silence`, `stall`, `no-input`, `cc-errors`, `loss` and `output-failed` as `event/direct.health`, only when the state or the set of alarm kinds changes, and answer `direct.thumbnail {show, width}` with a JPEG at the wall's width. A show whose rendition already decodes its input hands its pictures over from that decode instead. A show that composites judges its own programme (`crates/godwinmix-core/src/vitals/`): silence from the programme meter, black and freeze from the multiview's programme cell, `output-failed` and `shed` from the status, sent as the new `event/health`. Thresholds are per show with defaults, and a duration of zero switches a check off. Black and freeze for a show that only copies are good to about a second, or to the GOP when that is longer. On an M4 Pro, 200 shows of detailed 1080p with their alarms on cost 87 to 90% of one core with VideoToolbox and about 30 MB, and a wall of 40 thumbnails added nothing measurable; on the CPU alone two workers cap it at two cores and each show gets a picture about every two seconds. Tested against real black, frozen, silent, stalled and clean streams, each raising exactly its alarm and clearing it. See `docs/reference/show-health.md`.
* An output no longer stops for good while it says `live`. The scale harness caught every udp output of eight busy shows going silent, and a single show doing the same; the receivers were never the cause. The programme encoder starts on demand, and its `audiorate` filled the time from the start of the programme to its first buffer with silence, so the first output added a few seconds (or hours) in got that much audio stamped from zero ahead of video stamped from now. The udp sender's remuxer waits for both streams with a second of room each, so it stopped reading the programme; the core's write into the plugin's FIFO never returned; the overflow watchdog's reconnect then waited on that write for ever while holding the lock the output's state is read through, so the state stayed `live`, and removing the output held the mixer's command loop as well. Each link is fixed. The encoder's audio, and a rendition's, starts at its first buffer. The udp sender holds ten seconds of one stream while it waits for the other. A reconnect waits two seconds for the old pipeline, and for a sidecar output that stopped reading it stops the plugin, waits up to five more for the write to fail, and starts the plugin again on a fresh FIFO; a plugin process that died is started again the same way. `live` now also needs bytes to have reached the output's sink in the last three seconds, counted in a new `bytes_out` status field and in `gmx_output_bytes_total`, which was declared and never filled until now. A udp receiver that goes away and comes back is not a fault and causes no reconnect. Run again, the harness's eight show legacy run kept all eight outputs sending for the whole minute, with the longest silence 172 ms. See `docs/how-to/udp-and-multicast.md` and `docs/reference/plugin-lifecycle.md`.
* A harness for proving the mixer at headend scale. `dev/bench/scale.sh` sends 200 live MPEG-TS feeds made from four ten second clips (1080p, 720p, SD, and a two program multiplex), checks them at the generator, starts a release station from a copy of the example config, adds a show per feed with `show.add_many` (or `show.add` per row, or today's compositing shape with `--mode legacy`), samples the station and every process under it once a second along with `show.stats`, checks every output, and writes a report to `dev/bench/results/` with the machine, the version and the load average before the run. `--toggle` switches compositing on and off for one show and reports the gap on its output; `--format` asks for a rendition, for counting the transcodes the governor admits. It is all one Rust binary, `gmx-scale` in `tools/scale`, a workspace of its own with `libc` and `serde_json` as its only dependencies: a generator that paces each datagram by PCR and carries continuity counters, PCR, PTS and DTS on across every loop so a feed never restarts, with loss and jitter per feed; a checker that counts packets, continuity errors, PCR jumps and jitter, keyframes, GOPs dropped and silences on any number of UDP streams; and the bulk add, the sampler and the report. On an M4 Pro the generator sent 200 feeds at 1.025 Gbit/s over loopback unicast for 0.67 of one core and 28 MiB, and every feed arrived whole. Over loopback multicast macOS lost about a third of the same feeds in the kernel, so the script uses unicast unless told otherwise. The first runs, on the commit before direct shows, found two things to fix: an output whose queue fills is reconnected and never comes back while it still says `live`, and with eight busy shows some show and plugin processes were still running 15 seconds after the station stopped. See `docs/how-to/benchmark-at-scale.md`.
* The direct host: a show with compositing off runs in the ingest plugin's channel server, with no process of its own. The station hands it the table of such shows as `direct` (`dev/plans/wave4-direct-table.md`), every time whole; the host starts what is new, stops what went at once, and changes only the outputs that changed, so the others keep sending. Each show's input is published on the hub as `direct.<id>/main`, where the relay can hand it to a show that composites, and each output is one thread reading the hub straight, the restreamer's sender popping the hub reader's own bounded queue: a dead or slow output loses GOPs in its own queue and never slows the input or another output, which a test holds to a number. Outputs go to RTMP, RTMPS and SRT as channel destinations do, and now also to UDP unicast and multicast (`udp://239.2.2.2:5000?ttl=4&interface=en0`), RTP (`rtp://`), RIST (`rist://`) and a file (`file:///recordings/show.ts`), through an MPEG-TS muxer of the plugin's own (H.264, HEVC, AAC, AC-3, E-AC-3 and MPEG audio, nothing decoded) rather than a GStreamer pipeline per output; channel destinations take the same addresses, and SRT now goes through the same muxer, so an SRT destination carries what the inputs carry. An RTMP destination given AC-3 or MPEG audio sends the picture alone and says once that AAC needs a rendition. An output with a rendition is planned and admitted by the station as a channel's is and built by the same transcode code, one decode per show, its pairs published on the hub the relay serves so the station can package them for HLS (that station side is not written yet), and its decode hands one picture a second to the vitals. The host says `direct.input`, `direct.output` and, once a second, `direct.stats` for every show in one event, and answers `direct.stats` and `direct.thumbnail` as `tool.call`. `gmx-ingest --direct <table.json>` runs the host alone on a table file, read again when it changes, with a line of totals every five seconds. Measured on an M4 Pro with the machine otherwise busy: 50 shows, each a multicast MPEG-TS input at about 2.8 Mbit/s and a UDP multicast copy out, took 52% to 60% of one core and 42 to 58 MB for the whole host, about 140 Mbit/s in and out, with no GOP dropped, no continuity error and every frame of a recorded output present. See `docs/reference/direct-shows.md` and `docs/explanation/direct-host.md`.
* Every input a direct show can take, in `plugins/ingest/src/direct/input/`: MPEG-TS over UDP and RTP (unicast, or multicast joined on a named interface, source specific where the OS allows), SRT as caller or listener, RIST, RTSP over TCP or UDP, HLS and DASH, RTMP played from another server, a TS or MP4 file looped at its own pace, and a channel's stream read straight off the hub. Each hands on the hub's tags with nothing decoded, and reports `InputStats` once a second: state, rate, frame rate, size, codecs, audio channels, continuity errors, lost packets, keyframe interval and the age of the last frame, plus every program of a TS feed named from its SDT, so one can be chosen by number. A feed keeps going through loss and reconnects on its own, its time running on across each reconnect. An input can have a backup, kept warm and cut to at a keyframe when the main stalls, and back when the main has been steady. AC-3, E-AC-3 and MPEG layer II now ride in a `MediaTag` as enhanced RTMP v2 audio bodies, with no new field; MP3 keeps its classic FLV id. The udp plugin's TS parsing is now a library half the inputs share, and it counts continuity errors once per jump as `cc_errors` beside the packets lost. Tested against ffmpeg and GStreamer senders on loopback, what came out read back by ffprobe; at 1080p25 and 8 Mbit/s one input costs 0.7 to 3% of one core on an M4 Pro. See `docs/how-to/headend-feeds.md` and `docs/reference/direct-inputs.md`.
* A source restarting no longer stops the mixer answering. A stall restart of `cam-pulpit` on the example config held the command loop for more than five minutes, and every call in that time, a status read or a take, answered that the loop was held by `source.restart`. Restarting a source in place, stopping a removed one and reconnecting an output now run on a thread of their own; the mixer marks the source or output as busy, carries on, and hears back when the work is done. A restart is never run twice at once, a remove during a restart wins, and adding a source again under the same id waits for the old pipeline to stop (three seconds at most). A worker that runs past three seconds is named in the log. In tests, a source whose teardown is held for three seconds and an output whose reconnect is held for three seconds both leave status and takes answering in under 100 ms and the programme producing throughout.
* An output whose destination stops reading can no longer stop the programme. The cause of the restart above: with another core already on port 1935, the primary output's `rtmp2sink` waited for a handshake that never came, an allocation query from the video encoder waited in the output's feed queue behind it, the encoder and then the compositor waited on the encoder, and every source then looked stalled. The feed answers allocation and drain queries itself now, so nothing but buffers crosses to a destination, and a leaky queue drops those.
* A source removed and added again under the same name is no longer restarted for the one it replaced. A retry the supervisor had armed for the old source, or a restart of it still running on its own thread, used to land on the new one by name: in the recorded `source-stall` session a `hall` that could not connect was replaced by a working one, taken, and a second later put back to connecting on air. Each source now carries a generation and late work for an earlier one is dropped; an output's reconnect is matched to the output itself the same way. A source that keeps failing also reads as failed through its retries, where it had started reading connecting and failed in turn every few seconds once restarts left the mixer thread.
* An output whose address still carries a placeholder key (`YOUR-STREAM-KEY`, `your-key`, `change-me`) is attached and never dialled, so running the example config no longer knocks on YouTube and Facebook with a made up key on a loop. The Outputs panel already said **Needs a stream key** with **Add key**; the row has no **Reconnect** now, `output.reconnect` on it says to add the key, and the header says destinations need a key rather than that they are connecting. The outputs list no longer throws when a row changes shape, which it did the moment a destination got its key. See `docs/how-to/stream-to-a-platform.md`.

* Several shows on one machine. The process you start is now the station: it owns the control port and the page, the channels and the one ingest plugin, the governor and the list of shows, and it runs each show as a process of its own on a loopback port only it knows. `godwinmix --config godwinmix.toml` runs that config as the show `main` in place, so a single show setup is unchanged: same address, same files, same clients, and the station itself costs about 20 MiB and no measurable CPU. New methods `show.list`, `show.add` (empty, a copy of a show without its outputs, or a project file once `project.import` is here), `show.rename`, `show.remove`, `show.start` and `show.stop`, with `event/show.changed` and `event/show.removed`, all on `/api/v1/shows`. Every other call reaches one show, named with `?show=<id>` on `/rpc`, REST and the streams, or `show` in `core.subscribe`; with none it goes to `main`. The relay adds about 25 microseconds to a call and 30 to an event, and passes streams through without buffering; relaying the mosaic and the preview costs the station a tenth of a percent of one core. A show that dies is started again (three times at once, then the plugin host's backoff, failed after the seventh death in a row) and says so; in the kill test the other show recorded every frame. A show's renditions ask the station's governor over the show's link (`governor.admit`), and a show that dies gives its share back at once. Channel streams carry `relay`, so any show can take the stream the station received as a source. `--show main` runs one process with no station, as before. The page's show switcher is not in this change. See `docs/how-to/run-several-shows.md`, `docs/reference/shows.md` and `docs/explanation/architecture.md`.
* Shows as tabs on the page. After the menu bar, one tab per show: its name, a red dot when it is on air, and a small mark with a tooltip when it is starting, stopped or failed. One show is just its name and a `+`, so a single show setup looks as it did; several move to a row of their own under the live controls on a laptop screen and scroll sideways on a phone. A click switches the page to that show (it loads again with `?show=<id>`, which the socket, REST calls, snapshots, uploads, the composer's pictures and HLS links all carry; the layout stays). The `+` and File, New show make one empty, as a copy of this show or from a project file. Double click or `F2` renames in place; the tab's menu starts, stops and removes, with a yes first, and says why the last show and `main` stay. Removing the show the page is on goes over another show's line, because the station closes this one first, and a page whose show has gone moves to another. The row draws on every page and everything it does loads on the first click; a core without `show.*` gets no row. See `docs/how-to/run-several-shows.md` and `docs/reference/keyboard.md`.
* A routing view, View, Routing or the palette: every input on the machine (each channel's streams, each show's programme and sources) down the side, every output (channel destinations, each show's outputs) across, grouped by channel and show with sticky headers, a fold per group and a filter. Each cell says Copy, Programme encode, or the format with where it is encoded and its CPU cost from `rendition.plan`, or that a source reaches the output through the programme. An empty cell sends that stream to that destination in a format chosen with the Format step; a filled one opens its form; the `+` on a group adds a destination to that channel or show. Only the groups on screen are read, a show's over a socket of its own, and let go three seconds after they scroll away. On a phone it is a list of cards. No new method. See `docs/how-to/route-inputs-to-outputs.md`.
* A monitoring wall, View, Monitoring wall, the palette or the grid button after the show tabs: every show on the station as a row or a tile, with a picture every two seconds, the input's transport, size, frame rate, codec and bitrate over the last forty seconds with loss and CC errors, each output as a chip with its state, copy or format and bitrate, the encode load, each alarm with its age, and the compositing switch. The header counts every show, how many are live and in alarm, the total in and out, and the CPU and GPU from `governor.status`. Sort by any column, filter by words and by alarm, group by state. * A rendition planner, the new crate `godwinmix-render`. Given what every output asks for (container, codec, size, frame rate, bitrate, keyframe interval, audio) and what every source carries, `plan` returns the smallest graph that serves them all: a copy when the source already matches, one decode per source, one scale per distinct size and rate, one encoder per distinct shape shared by every output that wants it, and one keyframe interval across every rendition of a source so a ladder switches cleanly. Encoders are chosen hardware first, never on a device the cost model says is full, and each Encode node says why in a sentence ("using h264-software-x264 because the GPU nvidia0 is full"). * A resource governor, `crates/godwinmix-govern`, not wired into the mixer yet. It answers one question before anything starts: can this machine do this without taking a frame from what is on air? `Governor::admit(cost, what)` grants a ticket that holds a share of CPU, hardware encoder load and sessions, memory and uplink until it is dropped, or refuses with what the work needs, what is free and which renditions would fit (`1080p30 H.264 on h264-software-x264 fits, or 1080p60 H.264 on the GPU encoder h264-gpu`). What is free is worked out, not set: live load sampled once a second (`/proc` on Linux, Mach host statistics on macOS, `GetSystemTimes` on Windows), other programs counted at their recent peak, and a reserve sized for a desktop or a headless server, with an optional `reserve_cores` override. `shed()` says what to give up when the machine runs short on air: thumbnails and previews, then the lowest ladder rung upward, then faster software presets, never the programme or a live output's top rung. * A new first party plugin, `udp`, for MPEG-TS over UDP and RTP. `udp/source` receives a unicast port or a multicast group (`udp://@239.1.1.1:5000`, source specific as `udp://10.0.0.9@232.1.1.1:5000`), on the interface you name, and takes one program out of a multiplex by number or by PID, with the PAT and PMT rewritten to match. It drops null packets, tells bare TS and RTP apart by itself, counts lost packets from the continuity counters and RTP sequence numbers without ever waiting, and keeps listening through a sender that stops and starts again. Its health names the rate, the loss and every program in the feed. `udp/output` sends the programme as MPEG-TS, seven packets to a datagram, unicast or multicast, with a TTL, an interface, a DSCP mark and an optional constant bitrate padded with null packets; nothing is decoded or encoded. No port is open until a source or output starts. * A frame bus, the new crate `godwinmix-framebus`: one owner decodes a device or a stream once and any number of readers, in the same process or in others, read the decoded frames from shared memory with no copy. A reader that falls behind skips to the newest frame and never slows the owner or another reader; the owner never waits on a reader and drops a frame, counted, only if every slot is leased; a reader killed mid read leaves nothing locked, because the owner frees its leases when its socket closes; a reader keeps its frames when the owner dies and attaches to the next one by itself. Names are `camera:<id>` and `channel:<app>/<stream>`, found through a registry directory the station will own (`GODWINMIX_BUS_DIR`). NV12, I420, P010, YUY2, UYVY, BGRA, RGBA and BGRx at any size. For pipelines, `gmxbussink` and `gmxbussrc`, registered in process. Linux and macOS; on Windows it builds and says it cannot share yet. Nothing in the mixer uses it yet; shows will. `dev/framebus-bench.sh` measures it against every reader decoding for itself and against `unixfdsink`: at 1080p30 a reader costs about 0.2% of one core, so four shows reading one camera cost about one decode instead of four. Each reports its state, bit rate, reconnects and error back to the core, which shows them in `channel.list` and sends `event/channel.changed` whenever a destination is added, changed or removed, or its state moves. Destinations are kept with their channel in `<stem>.runtime.channels.toml`, their addresses and keys sealed in the secret store beside the channel's keys, and come back after a restart. * RTMP channels, the server half. A channel is a named place encoders publish to on the mixer's one RTMP port, `rtmp://<mixer>:1935/<app>/<stream>?psk=<key>`, and several streams can be live on one channel at once. Seven new methods: `channel.list`, `channel.get`, `channel.add`, `channel.set`, `channel.remove`, `channel.key.add` and `channel.key.remove`, and three events, `channel.changed`, `channel.removed` and `channel.refused`. Taking a key back or switching a channel off cuts off whoever is on air with it. A stream that goes live becomes a source called `<app>-<stream>` by itself, and goes again when the publisher leaves unless a scene holds it, in which case it waits on its freeze frame and comes back with the publisher. Each live stream reports its codec, size, frame rate and bit rate. Channels are kept in `<stem>.runtime.channels.toml` beside the runtime store and survive a restart. See `docs/reference/channels.md` and `docs/how-to/rtmp-channels.md`.
* The ingest plugin's `ingest/discover` is the channel server. Every stream goes through a hub where each reader has a bounded queue: a reader that falls behind loses whole GOPs from the front, counted as `dropped_gops`, and never slows the publisher or the other readers. A tag's payload is shared between readers, never copied per reader. A late reader gets `onMetaData` and the codec headers before its first keyframe. A mixer source reads its stream over loopback on the RTMP port itself, where it used to get a loopback port of its own per publisher; the old relay also wrote every publisher's bytes to every relay, and blocked the publisher on a slow reader. New `streams` tool. Eight 1080p30 6 Mbit/s publishers on three channels with two readers each cost the channel server about 4% of one core.

## 0.2.0 (2026-09-15)

The first GodwinMix release, grown from LiveboxMix 0.1.0.

* The core is a library (`godwinmix-core`) with a versioned protocol (`godwinmix-protocol`, api level 1) served over `/rpc` and `/api/v1`, with generated TypeScript, Python and Rust clients.
* Plugins are processes on a documented contract: a manifest, JSON lines on stdio, media over unixfd, shm or a pipe. Twelve first party plugins, an SDK, templates in five languages, a conformance harness, marketplaces with signed installs, and a WASM tier behind a feature.
* Scenes as a document the core owns, a slot pool on the compositor, transitions on control bindings, a composer in the web UI, layouts, presets, themes, an OBS importer and OGraf graphics.
* Nothing runs unless asked: the multiview, thumbnails, preview, audio monitoring and the encoder start with the first client and stop with the last. Idle core measured at 0.012 cores and 37 MB on an M4 Pro.
* Safety in the core, a compact agent surface with MCP over stdio and HTTP, hooks, session replay and an operator eval suite.
* A terminal UI, Companion, Stream Deck, OSC and tally integrations, a node daemon for remote placements over mTLS.
* Desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux with a trimmed GStreamer bundled; Docker image and a headless quickstart.

Known limits: Linux and Windows builds are verified by inspection and cross checks only until the project's CI runs; browser sources on macOS and Windows need the codec enabled CEF the project publishes; the second canvas is not built.
